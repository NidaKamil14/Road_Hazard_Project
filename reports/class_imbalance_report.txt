8. TASK 8: RECOMMEND BALANCING STRATEGY (STRICTLY TRAINING SPLIT)
================================================================================
The following evidence-based balancing recommendations are derived exclusively from the actual
training distribution (29,409 images, 57,739 instances):

1. Overrepresented Classes:
   - Class 1 (road_crack): Represents 59.08% of all training instances (34,113 boxes) and 53.18%
     of all training images (15,639 positive images). This class completely dominates loss gradients.
   - Class 3 (construction_barrier): Represents 32.73% of training instances (18,899 boxes),
     though concentrated in only 1,280 training images.

2. Underrepresented Classes:
   - Class 2 (waterlogging): Critically underrepresented. Contains only 99 training images and 99
     training instances (0.17% of training instances).
   - Class 0 (pothole): Moderately underrepresented. Contains 4,628 training instances (8.02%)
     across 2,599 training images (relative to 34,113 crack instances).

3. Controlled Downsampling Recommendation (HIGHLY APPROPRIATE):
   - Problem: Training on all 15,639 crack images causes the model to optimize almost entirely
     for crack detection at the expense of potholes and waterlogging. Furthermore, 11,299 empty
     background images in train (38.4%) causes excessive background gradient suppression.
   - Recommended Action:
     * Preserve ALL 1,507 training images containing BOTH pothole and road_crack.
     * Randomly downsample the 14,132 "crack-only" images to ~2,000–2,500 images.
       Result: Total training crack images = ~3,500–4,000 images (~7,500–8,500 crack instances).
     * Downsample the 11,299 background images to ~1,000 images (~10% background ratio, standard
       for Ultralytics YOLO).

4. Training-Time Augmentation Recommendation (HIGHLY APPROPRIATE):
   - Problem: Waterlogging has only 99 training samples, leading to high risk of overfitting.
   - Recommended Action:
     * Apply aggressive training-time Mosaic (p=1.0) and MixUp (p=0.15–0.25).
     * Random HSV jitter (hue=0.015, saturation=0.7, value=0.4) to simulate daylight, overcast,
       dusk, and night flood scenarios.
     * Scale jittering (0.5 to 1.5) and horizontal flip (p=0.5).
     * Caution: Avoid vertical flips (flips sky/ground geometry unrealistically) and excessive
       perspective shearing that distorts vertical construction panels.

5. Oversampling & Copy-Paste Recommendation (HIGHLY APPROPRIATE):
   - Problem: 99 waterlogging images will only appear once per epoch in standard sampling.
   - Recommended Action:
     * Instance-level Copy-Paste: Segment waterlogging surfaces from FloodDET and paste them onto
       asphalt roads in RDD2022 images to synthesize realistic multi-hazard scenes.
     * Dataset-Level Oversampling: Repeat/re-index the 99 waterlogging training images by a factor
       of 8x–10x (or apply a custom weighted sampler) so the model encounters ~800–1,000 waterlogging
       instances per epoch.
     * Repeat pothole-only images (1,092 images) by 1.5x–2.0x.

6. Additional Data Collection Recommendation (STRONGLY RECOMMENDED FOR PRODUCTION):
   - Even with 10x oversampling, 99 unique images cannot capture the full environmental variance
     of road waterlogging (reflection, puddle depth, urban vs highway, night glare).
   - Collecting or sourcing 1,000–2,000 additional real-world road waterlogging/puddle images
     (e.g., from rainy BDD100K driving sequences or open meteorological road databases) is the
     most effective path to production-grade generalization.

7. Suggested Target Balanced Training Distribution:
   By applying controlled downsampling of majority cracks and 8x oversampling of waterlogging:
   - Class 0 (pothole):              ~3,500 images | ~6,000 instances (~25% of training boxes)
   - Class 1 (road_crack):           ~3,500 images | ~7,500 instances (~30% of training boxes)
   - Class 2 (waterlogging):           ~800 images |   ~800 instances (~15% of training boxes)

   - Background images:              ~1,000 images |        0 instances
